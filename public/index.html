Your own document types

A template of your own is quickly built

For object types that are not shipped, or for fields that are missing. No scenario of your own as in Flexible Workflow, just a small class.

Way 1
Extend a shipped templateAppend the field to the template structure, inherit from the class, redefine one method. The new field is immediately available for title, rules and mail.
Way 2
Build your own templateUse the template class and define your own fields. That way every object type gets its template, including your own Z objects.
What way 1 looks like

One field added, one method redefined

1The new field, appended to the structure
2First the shipped template reads the document
3Then your own class fills its field
And that is itEnter the class as the template in the configuration. The new field appears in the field list and is ready for placeholders, rules and mail.

Part two · A real case

And in real life?
NIS2 supplier review

The same building blocks, one afternoon, a process that runs end to end.

Everything from here on is a real run in a real system. Every screenshot was checked against the database, not just against the screen.

The task

Check your suppliers —
as deeply as their criticality demands

Stage 1
Supplier responsibleChecks NIS2 relevance and the master data. Every reviewed supplier passes this stage.
Stage 2
Responsible buyerBusiness relationship, contracts, dependency. Only from criticality NIS2-2 upwards.
Stage 3
Security teamCertificates, audits, incidents. Reserved for the critical suppliers, NIS2-3.

Every stage records a result and a note. The last one is written back to the supplier.

The case decides

One definition, three ways through

Not relevant · no task at all
NIS2-1 · one stage
NIS2-3 · three stages

The branch is a condition on a background step, not a line of code. Change the field on the supplier and the same workflow behaves differently.

In the inbox

The task explains itself

Title with supplier number, name and criticality. Below it the supplier data, the job for this stage, and what the decision means — all from the maintenance dialog.

The text comes from a standard text (SO10) with placeholders; the template fills them from the supplier.

One click away

The document is attached, not described

Every task carries its document
One click opens the supplier

Which document, and what it is called, comes from two entries — the template and the object text.

Deciding

Result and note,
right where the decision happens

No second transaction, no attachment. The reviewer picks a result, types a note, and it is saved while typing.

Same panel in the SAP GUI, same data. Every change is logged with user, time and channel.

Nothing slips through

Approving without a result is not possible

No result · no approval
Critical · a note is required

The rule lives in one place and guards every channel: Fiori, SAP GUI and any API call.

Stage after stage

Every stage has
its own verdict

Stage 2 does not inherit what stage 1 said. The panel starts empty, and the buyer decides for themselves.

Everything that was said stays: the log keeps every stage, and the closing mail lists them one below the other.

When a stage is not convinced

Back one stage, with a reason

The reason is mandatory, not polite
Stage 1 returns — with what was entered before

The task goes back to the same person, not to a queue. Nobody starts from scratch.

Four eyes

Whoever decided once
does not decide again

The person who starts the review never gets their own task. And whoever decided stage 1 is out for stage 2 — even though they are a named agent there.

Two rows in the agent table, no code: WF_INITIATOR and WF_APPROVERS, both marked as excluded.

When nothing happens

A reminder first, the lead second

Deadline passed · reminder, higher priority
Still nothing · escalation to the NIS2 lead

Deadline, follow-up step and recipient are three fields. What was already typed stays — the lead sees the half-finished review, not a blank form.

The mails

Everyone is told,
in their own language

New task, reminder, escalation, returned, rejected, completed, failed. Seven situations, each with its own text — reason and history included.

The language follows the recipient, not the sender: the same rule sent one mail in German and one in English, in the same second.

The same rule, the same second

Everyone reads it
in their own language

Two recipients of the same mail rule, two languages. The language follows the recipient — not the person or the job that triggered the mail.

Subject and body come from standard texts. Where a translation is missing, the mail falls back instead of going out empty.

The point of the whole thing

The result lands
on the supplier

When the last stage is done, a background step writes the verdict into search term 2: NIS2 OK 18.09.26.

From there anyone can find it — in a list, in a report, in the next audit. Nobody has to open the workflow log.

When something goes wrong

Silence is the worst outcome

The starter hears about it
The log says exactly why

A locked business partner, a wrong key, a missing result: the workflow ends in a named end status, writes the reason to the application log and tells someone.
